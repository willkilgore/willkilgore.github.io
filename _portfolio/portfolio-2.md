## Objectives
Produce a lightweight and stiff computer mount for road biking with a reusable 3D printed mold, while balancing manufacturability for small-batch production

## Overview
Designed and evaluated different mount designs using Solidworks, then rapid prototype testing using an FDM printer and Cura slicer. Designs were iterated to maximize stiffness while minimizing excess material. Following this process, a multipart 3D-printable mold was designed and fabricated. A forged carbon fiber part was then manufactured with the geometry of the final prototype. Finally, the part was processed and finished to ensure resistance to the elements over its lifetime.

## Technical Details
- Multiple rapid FDM prototypes to test fit and shape
- Refined geometry to maximize stiffness-to-mass ratio while maintaining manufacturability with a reusable multi-part mold
- Mold is printed from PLA with a high infill and wall count to withstand clamping pressure
- Mold surface preparation and release agent selection minimized part defects and enabled repeatable demolding
- Forged carbon fiber is used for its relatively isotropic qualities and ability to fill the complex geometry of the mold
- Application of a clear coat reduces moisture ingress into the final part and improve lifespan
- Titanium hardware and anti-seize used to mitigate the galvanic corrosion of carbon fiber and aluminum contact
- Final part validated through static loading and on-bike testing
